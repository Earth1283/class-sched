Paste the whole semester. Done in ten seconds.

Type classes the way you'd say them, or paste rows straight from your registrar's page or a spreadsheet. A live preview shows what was understood before anything is added.

CS 101 Intro to Computing MWF 9:00-9:50 Room 204 MATH 240, Tue/Thu, 10:30-11:45, Hall B, Prof. Lee BIO 110 Lab Th 2-4:50pm Science 3
  • Guesses sensibly. “1-2:15” is the afternoon, “9-9:50” is the morning.
  • Says what's wrong. A line with no days or time is flagged, and the rest still import.
  • Skips duplicates already in your schedule.

Class Schedule

Starting from nothing is the easy part.

The first screen is a single question: how do you want to get your classes in? Paste them, import a .csv or .ics, add one by hand, or load a sample to look around.

Small things, done properly.

Click a class, see everything

Days, time, room and instructor in one calm popover. Right-click any slot, class or day for quick actions, and every delete comes with an Undo.

One Create button

A class, a pasted batch, or an assignment or exam. All on the keyboard too: Ctrl+N, Ctrl+B, Ctrl+T.

Today, on your desktop

An optional widget that sits quietly behind your other windows and shows today's classes, what's next, and what's due. Drag it anywhere, and it remembers the spot.

Close the app window and it keeps running. Right-click the widget to open the app or quit. It's off until you switch it on in Settings.

Reminders

A nudge before each class, and before assignments and exams are due, while the app or its widget is running. You pick how early.

Assignments and exams

“Coming up” lists what's due in the next two weeks. Overdue items turn red; tick them off when done.

Import and export

Bring in a CSV or .ics. Export an .ics to open in Google Calendar, Outlook or Apple Calendar.

Your terms, your look

Term dates, reminders, clock, week start, accent, density and more. See how Settings works.

It lives on your computer

No sign-in and no server. Your schedule is one JSON file, in %APPDATA%\ClassSchedule by default. Use Save schedule as… to keep it anywhere, like D:\ or a synced folder, and Open schedule file… to switch between terms.
